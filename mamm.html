Class
Mammalia

Overview
ETY

Etymology. From the Latin mamma, meaning breast — the origin of the class's name.

STA

Status in the animal kingdom. The largest group of vertebrates on Earth, and widely regarded as the most intelligent and advanced.

Live Specimen
Ornithorhynchus anatinus — platypus
External Anatomy & Physical Traits

Key Characteristics

  • Body
    Four regions. The body divides into head, neck, trunk, and tail (where present).
  • Coat
    Hair. A covering of hair or fur is present on every mammal, at some life stage.
  • Ears
    Pinnae. Mammals possess externally projecting ears — a trait unique to this class.
  • Move
    Locomotion. Typically achieved with two pairs of limbs — reduced to two, for walking, in humans.

Internal Physiology & Respiration

Key Systems

  • Breath
    Respiration. Mammals breathe using lungs, full stop.
  • Ocean
    Aquatic adaptation. Whales and dolphins have no gills — they must surface to draw air.
  • Heart
    Circulation. A four-chambered heart: two atria, two ventricles.
  • Temp
    Thermal regulation. Endothermic — internal body temperature stays constant regardless of surroundings.
Neural Development & Intelligence

The Mammalian Advantage

  • Brain
    Neocortex. The mammalian brain features a distinct outer region unseen in other vertebrate classes.
  • Role
    Function. This region governs higher-level thought, complex problem-solving, and advanced intelligence.

Reproduction & Early Life

Key Characteristics

  • Milk
    Nourishment. Mammalian mothers produce milk to suckle their young after birth.
  • Birth
    Live birth. Most mammals give birth to live young (viviparous) — monotremes are the exception.
  • Fert.
    Fertilization. Internal, with separate sexes (unisexual).

Reproduction & Early Life

Major Reproductive Subclasses

1. Monotremes (Egg-laying mammals)

Primitive mammals that lay eggs instead of giving live birth (e.g., Duck-billed platypus).

2. Marsupials (Pouched mammals)

Mammals that give birth to underdeveloped young, which finish developing inside a maternal pouch (e.g., Kangaroo).

3. Placentals (True viviparous mammals)

Mammals where young develop completely inside the mother's uterus fed via a placenta (e.g., Humans, Horses, Cows, Monkeys).

All Fun Facts & Trivia

Presentation Master List

Cetacea
The Giant of the Sea

Blue whales are the largest animals ever to exist — up to 200 tons, heavier than 30 adult elephants combined.

Circulation
Heart Like a Car

A blue whale's heart weighs as much as a small car — a person could swim through its largest arteries.

Anatomy
Unique Ear Flaps

External pinnae belong to mammals alone — birds, reptiles, and amphibians have ear openings but no flaps.

Cognition
Mirror-Smart Dolphins

Dolphins use the neocortex to recognise their own reflection and even wrap sponges around their noses as tools.

Monotremata
No Nipples for Platypuses

The platypus has mammary glands but no teats — it secretes milk directly through its skin, and babies lap it from the mother's fur.

Chiroptera
Only True Flying Mammal

Bats are the sole mammals capable of true, sustained flight — flying squirrels merely glide.
